I build machine-learning systems that have to survive contact with real infrastructure.

Constrained hardware, frozen SDK versions, and operators who need a decision, not a probability.

Currently working on computer vision and integration for a large surveillance deployment. Previously analytics at Saudia. BSc in Artificial Intelligence, University of Jeddah.

Four projects: one running against real infrastructure, three built to learn something specific. Each one links through to how it actually went, including the parts that didn't.

  • Camera cleanliness monitoring

    2025 — present · professional

    Detecting degraded camera feeds across a large deployment, and turning a model score into something an operator can act on. Runs on an offline inference server — a lightweight classifier exported to ONNX and loaded by a single .NET service with no runtime to vendor.

  • Arabic sentiment classification

    2025 · academic

    AraBERT fine-tuned over roughly 58,000 tweets, where dialect variance mattered more than architecture. The harder part wasn't the model — it was dialect variance and label disagreement that wasn't random.

  • Traffic signal control

    2024 · graduation project

    Replacing a fixed signal cycle with one that reads how many cars are actually waiting. The interesting number was recall — the baseline detector was missing half the vehicles, and in a counting system that becomes wrong timing.

  • Ticket classifier, containerised

    2026 — practice

    A deliberately small end-to-end service, built to put container fundamentals into practice. It builds and runs — it is not a production system, and it isn't presented as one.

I studied Artificial Intelligence at the University of Jeddah and graduated in 2025. Before that I worked in analytics at Saudia, which is where I first got comfortable with the gap between a model that works in a notebook and a system that has to run somewhere real.

Since graduating I've worked where models meet infrastructure — which in practice means Windows Server, restricted networks, and SDK versions that were frozen years ago. That constraint has shaped how I work more than any course did. When you can't reach the internet from the deployment machine, you learn to care about what a dependency actually costs.

Outside the day job I've worked through certifications in containers and Kubernetes with IBM, and in MLOps on Google Cloud. Some of that is now applied, some is still lab work, and I keep the two marked separately rather than blur them.

I write things down. Architecture, decisions, what broke and why. Partly for whoever inherits the system, partly because writing it down is how I find out whether I understood it.

Arabic is my first language. I work in English.
