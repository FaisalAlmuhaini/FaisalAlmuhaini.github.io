I studied Artificial Intelligence at the University of Jeddah and graduated in 2025. Before that I worked in analytics at Saudia, which is where I first got comfortable with the gap between a model that works in a notebook and a system that has to run somewhere real.

Since graduating I've worked where models meet infrastructure — which in practice means Windows Server, restricted networks, and SDK versions that were frozen years ago. That constraint has shaped how I work more than any course did. When you can't reach the internet from the deployment machine, you learn to care about what a dependency actually costs.

Outside the day job I've worked through certifications in containers and Kubernetes with IBM, and in MLOps on Google Cloud. Some of that is now applied, some is still lab work, and I keep the two marked separately rather than blur them.

I write things down. Architecture, decisions, what broke and why. Partly for whoever inherits the system, partly because writing it down is how I find out whether I understood it.

Arabic is my first language. I work in English.
